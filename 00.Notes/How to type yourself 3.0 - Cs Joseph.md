1. Open / Explosive Personality

(“Initiating” — Environment → Self)

(UDJM, UDPM, SDJF, SDPF)

(Initiating) is a willingness to start or lead a conversation and to change topics at any time.

Initiating is taking the active role to get to know others, reinforce bonds, or provide guidance.

- Initiates interaction
- “Explosive” = outward expression
- Cognitive development flows from environment toward self
- Linked to talking outwardly

Typology-aligned definition: An Open/Explosive personality projects energy, thoughts, and emotions outward first. Meaning is formed through engagement, not reflection.

**Key traits:**
- Initiates conversations and action
- Thinks by speaking
- Influences and shapes the environment
- Expresses emotions externally
- Learns through interaction

Psychology parallels

- Jung: Extraverted orientation
- Big Five: Higher Extraversion
- Communication style: Expressive / Initiator
- Temperament: Active, outwardly regulating

In short:

“I act or speak → then understand.”

____________

2. Closed / Implosive Personality

(“Responding” — Self → Environment)

(SDJM, SDPM, UDJF, UDPF)

(Responding) is a preference for a passive role in conversation and remaining on the topic.

Responding is waiting for others to initiate and relying on others to keep them in the loop.

- Responds rather than initiates
- “Implosive” = inward processing
- Cognitive development flows from self toward environment
- Linked to internal processing before speech

Typology-aligned definition: A Closed/Implosive personality absorbs, processes internally, then responds. Meaning is constructed privately before expression.

Key traits

- Reflective and reserved
- Thinks before speaking
- Conserves emotional energy
- Reacts selectively
- Learns through contemplation

Psychology parallels

- Jung: Introverted orientation
- Big Five: Lower Extraversion, higher Reflectiveness
- Communication style: Responder / Processor
- Temperament: Internally regulated

In short:

“I understand → then act or speak.”

_______________

3. Authentic Personality

(“Direct” — Self-aligned, not fitting in)

(UDJM, SDJM, SDJF, UDJF)

(Direct) communication is explicit, specific,concise, and forceful.

Direct leaves little doubt as to meaning or intent.

Direct chooses its own and other’s roles in conversation.

Direct is saying what you mean and

meaning what you say.

- “Never fitting in?”
- Direct expression
- Not seeking others to fit in
- Comfortable with discomfort

Typology-aligned definition: An Authentic personality prioritizes internal truth over social harmony. Expression is self-consistent, not adjusted to gain acceptance.

Key traits

- Direct communication
- High internal alignment
- Willing to stand apart
- Low social camouflage
- Accepts discomfort for honesty

Psychology parallels

- Humanistic psychology: Authenticity (Rogers)
- Big Five: High Openness, low Agreeableness masking
- Identity style: Self-authored
- Moral psychology: Principle-driven

In short:

“I stay true, even if I don’t belong.”

_______________

4. Harmonious Personality

(“Informative” — Fitting in)

(UDPM, SDPM, SDPF, UDPF)

(Informative) communication is implicit, wordy,descriptive, and vague.

Informative leaves open the possibility of

interpretation of meaning or intent to the

listener.

Informative provides maximum information without taking a stand on it and allows the listener to decide their role.

- Seeks others → to fit in
- Informative rather than direct
- Avoids discomfort
- Adjusts expression socially

Typology-aligned definition: A Harmonious personality prioritizes social cohesion and relational balance. Expression adapts to maintain comfort and inclusion.

Key traits

- Socially adaptive
- Reads the room well
- Values peace and belonging
- Modifies expression for harmony
- Avoids unnecessary friction

Psychology parallels

- Big Five: High Agreeableness
- Attachment theory: Relationship-preserving strategies
- Communication style: Diplomatic / Informative
- Social regulation: Externally attuned

In short:

“I adjust to belong.”
