### My redefinition / patch to Se vs Si

The first thing I want to develop in my argument is definitions. we previously stated something along this line:
- Si is about comfort, endurance, etc (curtailed to keep short)
- Se is about performance, reactions, etc

Now many of you have heard these definitions before. I am now going to state that one of the words chosen here is wrong, can you figure out which one? .... okay probably not. The reason why is all of these 4 words are pretty accurate. The word I have the most trouble with is performance for Se. 

My issue is the fact there are multiple definitions; which is the more colloquial definition of performing for others like a stage and an audience, lets call the 'the first definition':

`an act of staging or presenting a play, concert, or other form of entertainment. (Um ato de encenar ou apresentar uma peça, concerto ou outra forma de entretenimento)`

(Um ato de encenar ou apresentar uma peça, concerto ou outra forma de entretenimento)

Then there is the more formal / accurate definition of being able to do something, lets call this 'the second definition':

`the action or process of carrying out or accomplishing an action, task, or function.(a ação ou processo de realizar ou cumprir uma ação, tarefa ou função)`

Now, my entire argument stems on the fact that we tend to use one word, but can have two different meanings, which can make it very clearly difficult for precision. The way I see it, the first definition is more feminine. Example, woman will put on makeup and "put on a show" for men. The second definition is more masculine, example men have to work hard for their family and generally that is the traditional masculine gender role.

This definitional issue is extremely annoying. It makes it so there are two different things that both are being called "Se". Furthermore, one of them is inherently more extroverted, definition one, while the other is more introverted, definition two, because you yourself being able to do something is inherently introverted or self. In order to solve this, I created a discrepancy between these two definition, and apply one to Se and the other to Si:
